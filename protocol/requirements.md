# Requirements and acceptance record

Source: user instruction and the privately retained project brief, read in full on 2026-10-14 (Asia/Taipei).

## Required outcome

Identify researchable factors affecting classroom comprehension among deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) students using empirical evidence. Use an auditable C#/.NET pipeline to quantitatively evaluate the selected factors; the evidence determines the title, scope, and conclusions.

## Scope and decisions

- Search all eleven initial factors: captioning, sign-language support, real-time transcription, assistive listening devices, visual aids, teaching speed, written materials, teaching modality, background noise, seating position, and visual accessibility. Additional factors can be logged if retrieved.
- Select 1–3 factors only after examining independent samples, extractable statistics, outcome and comparison consistency, risk of bias, and undergraduate feasibility.
- Prefer at least five independent comparable studies per synthesis as a project target, not a statistical rule or proof of quality.
- Keep classroom/content comprehension distinct from isolated speech recognition, reading comprehension, perceived accessibility, cognitive load, and satisfaction. Near-scope evidence may inform feasibility but does not automatically enter the primary outcome synthesis.
- Record sample size, educational stage, design, conditions, measurement, means, standard deviations, or convertible statistics, with exact page/table provenance and missing values marked `NA`.
- Independent-group Hedges' g is conditional on the study design. Paired, clustered, observational, or repeated-outcome data require appropriate treatment and cannot be sent through the independent-groups formula.
- Implement descriptive statistics, valid effect-size standardization, and separate basic random-effects syntheses when defensible. Report confidence intervals and heterogeneity. Do not force pooling to satisfy the deliverable list.
- Do not implement machine learning, meta-regression, or advanced models.
- Validate C# numerical outputs against independent reference values. Python may optionally draw charts but must not replace C# study-data processing or statistical calculations.

## Six deliverables

1. Research evidence database containing the candidate factors, retained candidate publications, verification status, and retrieval/screening records.
2. Final factor-selection report grounded in availability, independence, comparability, quality, and feasibility.
3. Structured quantitative dataset from real original papers; missing statistics stay missing.
4. Reproducible C# statistical program and independent numerical validation evidence.
5. Factor-distribution chart, extracted-data and statistical-result tables, and forest plot when estimable effects exist.
6. A 2,000–3,000 English-word undergraduate paper, excluding references, in DOCX, PDF, and LaTeX. Use a consistent academic citation style and explicitly cite every evidence paper used; identify screened/excluded papers in a cited appendix if needed. Follow applicable PRISMA 2020 reporting and Cochrane statistical guidance.

## User's full-text gate

When original paper PDFs are needed, collect a substantial, prioritized batch of exact titles/DOIs/access links, pause, and wait for the user to purchase and supply them. This applies also to PDFs advertised as open access: do not silently download or read those PDFs. Abstracts and indexing/publisher metadata can be used for initial feasibility screening. Do not replace missing full text with invented statistics or an unsupported final paper. Resume after supplied PDFs arrive. Explain any explicit open-access labels so the user need not pay unnecessarily.
